Bottleneck Simulator

Two populations start out identical. The wild one stays large. The crop one is founded by a few plants, stays small for a while, then grows back. What does the bottleneck do to diversity, and to harmful mutations?

What to try

  1. 2 founders vs 50 founders.
  2. A short bottleneck (2 generations) vs a long one (40).
  3. Weak (0.001) vs strong (0.1) harmful mutations. Weak ones drift up in a small population; strong ones get purged anyway.

The point: in a small population, chance outweighs weak selection, so mildly harmful mutations can rise and even fix.

The model

Each generation, every plant is replaced by offspring of two parents; healthier parents are more likely to be picked. Each parent passes on one of its two copies of every site, at random. Every new gamete also picks up new harmful mutations (on average U), each at a new site; a plant with one copy loses h·sd of its fitness, with two copies sd, and costs multiply across sites. Before generation 0 the large population runs 200 generations so it already carries a realistic set of rare harmful mutations. The crop is then founded by F plants drawn from it.

Diversity is the chance that two random copies of a neutral site differ (60 neutral sites, averaged). The gene has 12 alleles at the start, from common to rare. Even the “large” population drifts a little: 500 plants is small next to a real wild species.
